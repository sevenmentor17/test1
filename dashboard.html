What is the Kraftshala Screening Test? And How to Crack the Kraftshala Screening Test The Kraftshala Screening Test is the first step in the admission process for many of Kraftshala’s flagship programs. Its purpose is to identify candidates who have the potential to succeed in a fast-paced learning environment. The screening process generally evaluates: Analytical thinking Logical reasoning Communication skills English comprehension Business aptitude Learning mindset Problem-solving ability Unlike many competitive exams, it doesn’t require months of preparation or advanced technical knowledge. Is the Kraftshala Screening Test Hard? This is probably the most searched question. My answer is: No—but only if you prepare smartly. Many students think they’ll be asked difficult digital marketing questions. That’s not true. Most questions focus on how you think rather than how much you already know. If you have decent English, basic logical reasoning skills, and can analyze situations calmly, you’re already in a strong position. The biggest challenge isn’t the difficulty level—it’s managing your time and staying focused throughout the assessment. My Experience Preparing for the Kraftshala Screening Test When I first decided to apply, I started searching YouTube videos and Reddit discussions. Honestly, there wasn’t much detailed information available. So I decided to prepare using three simple methods: Solving aptitude questions Reading business articles daily Practicing logical reasoning I also refreshed my basic communication skills and spent time understanding how businesses think. That made a huge difference during the assessment. Understanding the Screening Test Pattern While the exact format can change over time, the assessment usually includes questions related to: 1. Logical Reasoning You’ll encounter questions that test how well you identify patterns, solve problems, and think critically. Examples include: Number patterns Logical puzzles Sequence questions Analytical reasoning The key is accuracy rather than speed. 2. English Comprehension Communication is a major part of marketing. Expect questions like: Reading comprehension Vocabulary Grammar Sentence correction Paragraph understanding Reading English articles regularly can significantly improve your performance. 3. Quantitative Aptitude Don’t worry. The math is generally basic. Topics may include: Percentages Ratios Profit and loss Averages Basic calculations Practicing these fundamentals is usually enough. 4. Business and Marketing Awareness This doesn’t mean you need years of marketing experience. Instead, understand: How businesses make money What advertising does Why brands market products Customer behavior Basic digital marketing concepts Reading blogs about marketing can give you a solid foundation. Best Preparation Strategy Here’s the preparation strategy that worked well for me. Step 1: Improve Logical Thinking Spend 30–45 minutes daily solving reasoning questions. Good topics include: Blood relations Coding-decoding Puzzles Seating arrangements Series Logical deductions Consistency matters more than studying for long hours. Step 2: Read Every Day Read articles from marketing and business websites. Focus on understanding: Consumer behavior Advertising campaigns Startup stories Marketing strategies This improves both comprehension and business awareness. Step 3: Practice Aptitude Revise topics such as: Percentages Time and work Speed, distance, and time Ratio and proportion Data interpretation Even 20–30 minutes of daily practice can make a noticeable difference. Step 4: Develop a Marketing Mindset One thing I realized after joining Kraftshala is that marketers constantly ask questions like: Why would someone buy this product? Who is the target audience? What problem does this solve? Which platform should we advertise on? Start thinking this way while preparing. It helps far beyond the screening test. Common Mistakes Students Make Here are mistakes I noticed many aspirants make: Waiting Until the Last Minute Preparation doesn’t require months, but don’t leave everything until the night before. Ignoring English Many students focus only on aptitude. However, communication plays a huge role in marketing. Reading regularly improves comprehension and confidence. Panicking During the Test Some questions are intentionally designed to test your thinking process. If you get stuck, move ahead and return later. Time management is essential. Overthinking Every Question Not every question has a trick. Sometimes the simplest answer is the correct one. Stay calm.
